Plain-English terms used below (first use):
- 0DTE: an option that expires the same day it is bought.
- ATM: "at the money", the option whose strike price is nearest the current price.
- Pre-registration: writing down the test, the pass/fail rule and the sample before looking at any result.
- Holm correction: a way of raising the bar for "significant" when many tests are run at once, so one lucky result among many does not count.
- p-value: the chance of seeing a result this strong from pure luck. Under 0.05 is the usual "maybe real" line.
- CI (confidence interval): the range the true number plausibly sits in. If it includes zero, the effect might be nothing.
- Null: a test that found nothing.
- bp (basis point): one hundredth of a percent. 1 bp on a 700-dollar stock is 7 cents.
- sigma (σ): a unit of "how big a move is compared to normal wobble".
- Lookahead: using information that was not yet knowable at the moment of the decision. It makes backtests look far better than reality.
- Underlying twin: in the live lab, the same trade measured on the stock/ETF itself (QQQ or SPY) over the same minutes, so you can tell "the chart direction was wrong" apart from "direction was right but the option lost money anyway".
- IMB: the setup called IntradayMomentumBoundary (a published Zarattini/Aziz/Barbon rule).
- MOMO_CHASE: the lab's coded reconstruction of the trader's own discretionary habit (buy after a momentum burst).
